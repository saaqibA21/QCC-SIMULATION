🛡️ MDI-QKD vs. Standard BB84: Detector-Attack Immunity

Testing the Detector-Blinding Attack (Lydersen et al. 2010) on trusted detectors (BB84) vs an untrusted relay (MDI-QKD).

BB84 VULNERABILITY

STANDARD BB84 (Detector-Blinded)

Hardware Loophole: Eve blinds Bob's SPADs with CW laser into linear regime, then triggers deterministic clicks with classical pulses.
Bob Measured QBER: 0.0% (Stealth!)
Eve Stolen Key: 100.0% LEAKED
QBER Alarm: NOT TRIGGERED
Security Verdict: COMPROMISED
FATAL BREACH: Bob's QBER remains below threshold (0%). Alice & Bob trust the key, while Eve holds an exact copy!
MDI-QKD DEFENSE

MDI-QKD (Untrusted BSM Relay)

Architectural Fix: Alice & Bob transmit to untrusted Charlie relay. Charlie only announces Bell states (|ψ⁻⟩). No detector trust required!
Charlie Output: |ψ⁻⟩ Parity Only
Eve Stolen Key: 0.0% (ZERO LEAK)
Relay Trust Req: 0% (UNTABLE RELAY)
Security Verdict: 100% IMMUNE
PERFECT SECURITY: Even if Eve controls Charlie's detectors completely, she only learns mutual parity, giving 0 bits of Alice or Bob's key!

PROTOCOL SECURITY MATRIX (BB84 VS. MDI-QKD)

Security Property Standard BB84 MDI-QKD (Lo et al. 2012) MDI-QKD Architectural Advantage
Detector Trust Assumption Requires 100% Trusted SPADs 0% Trust in Detectors (Untrusted Relay) All measurement devices placed at relay; Alice and Bob only possess transmitters.
Detector-Blinding Attack Vulnerable (Key Leaks at QBER ≈ 0%) Mathematically Immune Relay output is a Bell-state projection; individual bit values are never measured.
Time-Shift & Efficiency Mismatch Exploitable Side-Channel Immune to All Detector Flaws Closes all known and unknown detector side-channels simultaneously.

📋 Methodology & Plan of Action (From Presentation Slide 7)

01

Generate Random Bits

Alice generates a random binary bit sequence $A \in \{0, 1\}^N$ using quantum or cryptographic random number generation.

02

Select Random Bases for Alice

Alice chooses randomly between Rectilinear ($+$) and Diagonal ($\times$) bases for each photon.

03

Encode Qubits

Photons are prepared in $|0\rangle, |1\rangle, |+\rangle, |-\rangle$ polarization angles ($0^\circ, 90^\circ, 45^\circ, 135^\circ$).

04

Simulate Quantum Transmission

Single photons propagate across optical fiber / free space subject to environmental attenuation and noise.

05

Introduce Eve (Intercept & Resend)

Eve intercepts qubits, measures in a random basis, collapses the wavefunction, and sends a new photon to Bob.

06

Bob Measures Using Random Bases

Bob independently selects random bases ($+$ or $\times$) and records single-photon detector clicks.

07

Sift Matching Bases

Alice and Bob announce bases over classical channel; bits with mismatched bases are discarded (~50%).

08

Compare Sample Bits

Alice and Bob publicly reveal a random test fraction (33%) of the sifted key to estimate channel integrity.

09

Calculate QBER

Compute error rate: $\text{QBER} = \frac{\text{errors observed}}{\text{tested sample bits}}$.

10

Detect Possible Eavesdropping

Compare QBER against 11% threshold: Abort if exceeded, or distill secret key via privacy amplification.

📚 Literature Review: Foundations of MDI-QKD & Attack Immunity

RESEARCH GAP IDENTIFIED (From Project Presentation)

Existing literature largely optimizes MDI-QKD's key rate and distance in isolation — very few accessible simulation studies directly demonstrate, side-by-side, why the protocol is needed: a working detector-side attack (detector-blinding / time-shift) that breaks BB84's security assumption and is neutralized by MDI-QKD's untrusted-relay architecture. This project fills that gap with a reproducible, attack-vs-defense simulation.

1. Li, W. & Zhao, S. (2018)

"High-Dimensional Measurement-Device-Independent Quantum Key Distribution Based on Spatial Basis", arXiv:1806.00560.

Proposes encoding qudits in spatial (position/momentum) degrees of freedom to push MDI-QKD's secret key rate closer to that of standard BB84, addressing historically low throughput.

2. Ye, W., Zhong, H., Wu, X., Hu, L. & Guo, Y. (2019)

"Continuous-Variable Measurement-Device-Independent QKD via Quantum Catalysis", arXiv:1907.03383.

Uses zero-photon catalysis to extend achievable transmission distance and secret key rate of continuous-variable MDI-QKD, showing improved tolerance to detector imperfections.

3. Jing, F., Liu, W., Kong, L. & He, C. (2021)

"Improving the Performance of CV-MDI-QKD via a Noiseless Linear Amplifier", Entropy 23(12), 1691.

Adds a noiseless linear amplifier at the untrusted relay's detection stage, demonstrating gains in both communication distance and key rate under realistic channel excess noise.

4. Neumann, S. P. et al. (2021)

"Gigahertz Measurement-Device-Independent Quantum Key Distribution Using Directly Modulated Lasers", npj Quantum Information 7, 121.

Demonstrates a practical, high-speed MDI-QKD system reaching secure key rates an order of magnitude above prior art, proving real-world feasibility at gigahertz clock rates.

5. Wang, Y. et al. (2025)

"Advances of Quantum Key Distribution and Network Nonlocality", Review article, PMC12468356.

Surveys progress across CV-MDI-QKD, TF-QKD, and asynchronous MDI-QKD, positioning MDI protocols as the practical bridge between device-dependent QKD and full DI-QKD.

6. Lo, H.-K., Curty, M. & Qi, B. (2012) — Foundation

"Measurement-Device-Independent Quantum Key Distribution", Phys. Rev. Lett. 108, 130503.

The landmark paper inventing MDI-QKD: proves that placing the measurement device at an untrusted relay completely eliminates all detector side-channel vulnerabilities.
