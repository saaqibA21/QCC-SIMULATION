📈 QBER vs. Interception Fraction Curve (arXiv:2603.27278 Review)

As validated in literature (Lee et al. 2023 & arXiv:2603.27278), the Quantum Bit Error Rate rises near-linearly with Eve's interception fraction $f$, saturating at ~25% under full intercept-and-resend: QBER(f) = 0.25 × f + e_channel

📋 Methodology & Plan of Action (From Presentation Slide 7)

01

Generate Random Bits

Alice generates a random binary bit sequence $A \in \{0, 1\}^N$ using quantum or cryptographic random number generation.

02

Select Random Bases for Alice

Alice chooses randomly between Rectilinear ($+$) and Diagonal ($\times$) bases for each photon.

03

Encode Qubits

Photons are prepared in $|0\rangle, |1\rangle, |+\rangle, |-\rangle$ polarization angles ($0^\circ, 90^\circ, 45^\circ, 135^\circ$).

04

Simulate Quantum Transmission

Single photons propagate across optical fiber / free space subject to environmental attenuation and noise.

05

Introduce Eve (Intercept & Resend)

Eve intercepts qubits, measures in a random basis, collapses the wavefunction, and sends a new photon to Bob.

06

Bob Measures Using Random Bases

Bob independently selects random bases ($+$ or $\times$) and records single-photon detector clicks.

07

Sift Matching Bases

Alice and Bob announce bases over classical channel; bits with mismatched bases are discarded (~50%).

08

Compare Sample Bits

Alice and Bob publicly reveal a random test fraction (33%) of the sifted key to estimate channel integrity.

09

Calculate QBER

Compute error rate: $\text{QBER} = \frac{\text{errors observed}}{\text{tested sample bits}}$.

10

Detect Possible Eavesdropping

Compare QBER against 11% threshold: Abort if exceeded, or distill secret key via privacy amplification.

📚 Literature Survey & Academic Foundations

1. Bennett & Brassard (1984)

"Quantum Cryptography: Public Key Distribution and Coin Tossing", Proc. IEEE Int. Conf. on Computers, Systems and Signal Processing.

The original seminal paper that introduced the BB84 protocol and conjugate photon bases $\{+, \times\}$.

2. Shor & Preskill (2000)

"Simple Proof of Security of the BB84 Quantum Key Distribution Protocol", Phys. Rev. Lett. 85, 441.

Provided the first rigorous, unconditional security proof for BB84 using Calderbank-Shor-Steane (CSS) quantum codes, establishing the 11.0% QBER bound.

3. Gisin, Ribordy, Tittel & Zbinden (2002)

"Quantum Cryptography", Reviews of Modern Physics 74, 145.

A comprehensive benchmark review covering BB84 theory, intercept-resend eavesdropping strategies, and experimental QKD implementations.

4. Mina & Simion (2020)

"A Scalable Simulation of the BB84 Protocol Involving Eavesdropping", IACR ePrint 2020/1074.

Demonstrated Qiskit-based BB84 simulation of the intercept-resend attack and statistical eavesdropping estimation.

5. Lee, Lee & Lee (2023)

"Analysis on Eavesdropper Detection in BB84 QKD Protocol against Partial Intercept-and-Resend Attack", IFIP ONDM 2023.

Analyzed QBER-threshold detection algorithms for noisy optical channels under partial interception fractions.

6. QBER Analysis Review (2026)

"Quantum Bit Error Rate Analysis in BB84 QKD: Measurement, Statistical Estimation, and Eavesdropping Detection", arXiv:2603.27278.

Confirms that QBER rises near-linearly with interception fraction, saturating at ~25% under full intercept-and-resend.
